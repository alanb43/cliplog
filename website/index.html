How it works

1

Install & forget

Cliplog sits in your menu bar or system tray and quietly remembers everything you copy.

2

Press your shortcut

The default is ⌘⇧V on Mac and CtrlShiftV on Windows. You can change it in Settings.

3

Click to copy

Your 25 latest clips appear. Click one (or press ↵) and it’s back on your clipboard, ready to paste. You can also search, or press “Show more”.

What Cliplog remembers

Text

Plain or formatted. Bold, links and tables stay intact when you paste somewhere that supports them.

Images & screenshots

Shown as thumbnails. Click one and paste the full image anywhere.

Files & app content

Files from Finder or Explorer, plus app-specific content like Figma layers or Excel cells. It pastes back just like the original.

About copied files: copying a file normally copies its location, not the file itself. For small files (up to 32 KB), Cliplog saves its own copy, so pasting still works after the original is moved or deleted. For bigger files it keeps only the location. If you move, rename or delete one of those, pasting that clip won’t find it.

You choose how many clips to keep and how much disk space Cliplog may use. The oldest clips are removed first. Passwords copied from password managers are never saved.

Opening Cliplog for the first time

Cliplog is a free open source project, so it isn’t signed with a paid Apple or Microsoft certificate yet. Your computer will ask you to confirm the installation.

Mac

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg and drag Cliplog into Applications.
  2. Open Cliplog. If macOS says it can’t verify the app, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway.
  3. If it says the app is “damaged”, run this once in Terminal:
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Cliplog.app

Windows

  1. Run the downloaded -setup.exe.
  2. If you see “Windows protected your PC”, click More info → Run anyway.
  3. Cliplog starts in the system tray. If the icon isn’t visible, click the ^ arrow next to the clock.

Private by design

Your clipboard history stays on your computer, in your user folder. Cliplog has no accounts, analytics or network access. The source code is open, so anyone can check.
